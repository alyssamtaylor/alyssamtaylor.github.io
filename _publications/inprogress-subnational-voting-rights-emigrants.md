## Working Abstract

While scholarly attention has focused more on attitudes towards including immigrants in the demos, we know less about how resident citizens evaluate emigrants' belonging. Existing research has largely asked whether emigrants should be included or excluded (Michel and Blatter 2021), how resident and emigrant citizens value external voting (Himmelroos and Peltoniemi 2021), and what persuades support for it (Himmelroos et al. 2025). This work has not examined subnational enfranchisement. In the US and Switzerland, where electoral policy falls under regional jurisdiction, emigrant citizens’ enfranchisement in local and regional elections varies, so some citizens share subnational decision-making power with those who left, while others do not. I ask whether subnational enfranchisement of emigrants is associated with resident citizens' views on which emigrants belong to the demos. I use a vignette experiment (~1500 respondents each in the US and Switzerland, 2024), varying attributes of citizens abroad (e.g., years abroad, home-country links) and measuring support for external subnational voting rights. Subgroup analyses assess whether living in jurisdictions that enfranchise emigrants subnationally is associated with how respondents consider who belongs to the demos.
