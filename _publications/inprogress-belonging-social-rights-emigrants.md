## Working Abstract

While attitudes towards immigrants and their access and deservingness of rights are being widely studied (van Oorschot 2006; Reeskens & van der Meer 2019, Gandenberger 2023; Hainmueller & Hopkins 2015), we know little about attitudes towards emigrants—in particular, their access to social rights (e.g., access to public assistance in the origin country). Related works consider whether citizens sanction emigrants through disenfranchisement (Roseberg & Wejryd 2022) and whether recently returned citizens are viewed as less deserving of welfare state access (Gandenberger 2023). This is relavent because understanding what conditions impact resident citizens’ willingness to deny or give access nonresident citizens—despite democracies’ overarching desire to retain these citizens—is an important step in understanding how resident citizens envision belonging and where the limits lie.

In this paper, we provide insights into how citizens in the three countries prefer to share their privileges with those who left. We use a vignette survey experiment embedded in a 2024 survey fielded in Switzerland, Sweden, and the US, with approx. 1500 respondents per country, varying attributes of citizens abroad (e.g., years since they left, link to the home country) and measuring support for the inclusion in welfare state services (i.e., unemployment, social aid, and healthcare). The design identifies which characteristics of emigrant citizens shape resident citizens’ willingness to share welfare state privileges with in-group members who left.
