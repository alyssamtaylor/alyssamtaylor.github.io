## Working Abstract

While surveys have measured attitudes towards immigrants by asking about their impact on society, respondents imagine different immigrants when asked these questions (Blinder, 2015; Sidler et al., 2024). This is relevant because the immigrant an individual conjures in their mind may affect their willingness to extend social and political rights to the “imaginary immigrant”. In this paper, we ask which “imagined immigrants” respondents have in mind, who imagines them, and whether the imagined immigrant is associated with respondents’ preferred access to immigrants’ social and political rights.

This planned analysis uses a 2024 survey fielded in Sweden, Switzerland, and the US with approx. 1500 respondents each. After two batteries of questions on immigrants’ access to political and social rights, an open-ended question asked respondents who they were thinking about when they read the term ‘immigrants. Responses will be classified using an appropriate text analysis model to categorize who the imagined immigrants are. We expect to see immigrants grouped into categories based on their reason for migration, country or region of origin, religion, legal status, and temporality. Latent profile analysis will then identify which respondents imagine each category. Finally, we will estimate the relationship between the categorization of the “imagined immigrant” and the preferred access to social and political rights be attributed.
