With J. Chueri, M. K. Gandenberger, C. M. Knotz, and F. Fossati.

## Abstract

Research on the redistributive position of populist radical-right parties (PRRPs) describes them as welfare chauvinists and authoritarian: they support a generous welfare state exclusively for nationals, and specifically ‘hard-working’ nationals. However, it is unclear whether the welfare preferences of PRRP voters (the demand side) mirror the parties’ positions. Research focusing on the demand side has found an ambivalent relationship with welfare generosity and suggested an unsettled tension between PRRP voters’ preference for a generous welfare state and that for excluding claimants they view as ‘undeserving.’ This study examines the redistributive preferences of these voters through a factorial vignette survey experiment that revels the relationship between deservingness perception and welfare solidarity. We find that PRRP voters do not have more authoritarian welfare preferences than other voters, and that their most distinctive welfare attitude is that they are particularly exclusionary toward foreigners. However, they do not support a generous welfare state, even for those considered to be most deserving. These results challenge the literature’s understanding of the welfare preferences of PRRPs voters, giving a new perspective to studies on economic motivations for PRRPs voting.
